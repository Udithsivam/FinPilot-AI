A budget is a plan for how you will spend your money each month. It compares
your income against your expenses over a fixed period, typically a month,
so you can see whether you are spending more than you earn before it
becomes a problem.

A common starting framework is the 50/30/20 split: roughly 50% of
after-tax income toward needs (housing, groceries, utilities, minimum
debt payments), 30% toward wants (dining out, entertainment,
subscriptions), and 20% toward savings and extra debt repayment. This is
a starting heuristic, not a rule — actual proportions should reflect your
own income level, cost of living, and obligations.

Zero-based budgeting is an alternative approach where every unit of
income is assigned a job (expense, saving, or debt repayment) until
nothing is left unassigned. This tends to surface small, easy-to-ignore
recurring costs that a percentage-based budget can miss.

Reviewing a budget regularly — monthly is common — lets you catch
categories that are drifting upward before they become a large,
entrenched habit.
