Financial risk broadly refers to the chance that an outcome differs from
what you planned for — an expense is larger than expected, an income
source disappears, or an investment loses value. Managing personal
financial risk generally combines a few tools together rather than
relying on one: an emergency fund for short-term shocks, insurance for
large/rare losses, and diversification for investment risk.

Risk tolerance is how much loss or volatility someone is psychologically
and financially able to accept, and it's specific to the individual —
someone with stable income and few dependents can typically absorb more
investment volatility than someone supporting a household on variable
income.

Concentration risk is the risk of having too much exposure to a single
source — one employer, one stock, one asset class, or one country's
economy. Reducing concentration (across income sources, investments, or
both) is a common way to reduce the impact of any single point of
failure.

Not all risk can or should be eliminated — some risk is the necessary
tradeoff for return (see investing_terminology). The goal of risk
management is usually to avoid *ruinous* risk (a single event that
would be catastrophic) rather than to avoid risk entirely.
