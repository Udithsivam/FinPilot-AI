Insurance transfers the financial risk of a large, unpredictable loss
(a serious illness, a totaled car, a destroyed home) from an individual
to an insurer, in exchange for a smaller, predictable, regular cost (the
premium). The core tradeoff is paying a known small amount to avoid an
unknown potentially large one.

A deductible is the amount you pay out of pocket before insurance
coverage kicks in; a higher deductible typically means a lower premium,
since you're absorbing more of the small/medium losses yourself in
exchange for cheaper protection against large ones.

Common categories of personal insurance include health, life,
disability/income protection, auto, home/renters, and liability
coverage. Which of these matter most depends heavily on individual
circumstances — for example, income-protection style coverage matters
more for someone whose household depends on their income continuing.

Being under-insured (or having no coverage at all) in one of these areas
means a single adverse event can undo years of saving; being
over-insured means paying ongoing premiums for protection that may not
match your actual risk exposure. Periodically reviewing coverage against
current circumstances (income, dependents, assets) is a reasonable
practice.
