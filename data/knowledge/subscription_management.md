Subscriptions (streaming, software, memberships) tend to accumulate
gradually — each one looks small individually, so they rarely trigger
the same scrutiny as a single large purchase, but their combined monthly
cost can become a meaningful share of discretionary spending.

A periodic audit — listing every active subscription along with its cost
and how recently it was actually used — is a common way to surface ones
that are no longer providing value. Anything unused in the last billing
cycle or two is a natural candidate to cancel.

Many subscriptions auto-renew by default and are easier to start than to
stop (requiring a cancellation flow rather than automatically lapsing),
which is why deliberate periodic review matters more for subscriptions
than for one-time purchases.

Grouping subscriptions by category (entertainment, productivity,
fitness, etc.) can reveal redundancy — for example, multiple overlapping
streaming or productivity tools covering the same need — that isn't
obvious when reviewing charges individually.
