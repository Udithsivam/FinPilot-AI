Savings rate is the share of income that is set aside rather than spent,
usually expressed as a percentage: (income - expenses) / income.
Tracking this rate over time is often more informative than tracking
the absolute amount saved, since it adjusts naturally for changes in
income.

"Pay yourself first" is a common savings heuristic: treating savings as
a fixed obligation deducted at the start of the month (often
automatically, via a standing transfer) rather than whatever happens to
be left over after discretionary spending. This tends to produce more
consistent saving than a "spend first, save what's left" approach.

Savings goals are more actionable when they're specific and time-bound
(a target amount and a target date) rather than open-ended, since that
lets you calculate a concrete required monthly contribution and track
progress against it.

A declining savings rate over consecutive months, even without any
single large expense, is often an early signal that recurring costs
have crept upward and are worth reviewing category by category.
