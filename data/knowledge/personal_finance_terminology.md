Disposable income is income remaining after mandatory deductions (taxes,
required contributions) — what's actually available to spend or save.
It differs from discretionary income, which is disposable income minus
essential living costs (housing, food, utilities, minimum debt
payments) — i.e. what's left for non-essential spending and additional
saving.

Net worth is total assets (what you own: cash, investments, property)
minus total liabilities (what you owe: loans, credit card balances). It
is a snapshot at a point in time, unlike cash flow, which is measured
over a period (see cash_flow).

A recurring expense is one that repeats on a predictable schedule (rent,
subscriptions, loan EMIs), as opposed to a one-off or variable expense.
Recurring expenses are worth identifying separately because, in
aggregate, they represent a largely fixed monthly commitment that
constrains how much flexibility exists in a budget.

Liquidity refers to how quickly and easily an asset can be converted to
cash without losing significant value — cash itself is maximally liquid,
while assets like real estate are illiquid. Emergency funds are kept
liquid specifically because the point is fast access, not growth.
