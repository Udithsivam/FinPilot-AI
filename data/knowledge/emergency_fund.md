An emergency fund is money set aside specifically to cover unplanned
expenses or a loss of income — a medical bill, a car repair, or a job
loss — without having to rely on high-interest debt like credit cards.

A commonly cited target is 3 to 6 months of essential living expenses
(not full income — just what you need to cover rent/mortgage, food,
utilities, insurance, and minimum debt payments). Someone with variable
income (freelance, commission-based, self-employed) or a single-income
household is often advised to aim toward the higher end of that range,
since their income is less predictable.

Emergency funds are typically kept in an account that is safe and liquid
— easy to access without penalty — rather than invested in the stock
market, because the point is availability, not growth. A high-yield
savings account is commonly recommended for this reason.

Building the fund gradually (a fixed amount or percentage set aside each
month) is more sustainable for most people than waiting until there's a
large lump sum available.
