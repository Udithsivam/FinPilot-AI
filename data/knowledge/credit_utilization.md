Credit utilization is the percentage of your available revolving credit
(mainly credit cards) that you're currently using. It's calculated as
total balances divided by total credit limits.

It's one of the more heavily weighted factors in commonly used credit
scoring models. As a general guideline, keeping utilization under
roughly 30% is often cited as favorable, and lower is generally better
for the score, though the exact effect varies by scoring model and
individual credit history.

Utilization is typically calculated from the balance reported to the
credit bureaus on your statement closing date — not necessarily the
balance right before you pay it off — so paying down a card before the
statement closes (rather than just before the due date) can lower the
utilization that gets reported.

Because utilization is based on both numerator (balance) and denominator
(limit), it can rise either from spending more or from a credit limit
being reduced, even without any change in spending behavior.
