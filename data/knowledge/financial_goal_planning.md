Financial goals are generally more achievable when they're SMART:
Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. "Save more"
is a wish; "save ₹100,000 for an emergency fund by December" is a goal
that can be broken into a concrete monthly contribution.

Short-term goals (under 1 year — a vacation, a gadget), medium-term
goals (1–5 years — a down payment, a wedding), and long-term goals
(5+ years — retirement) usually call for different vehicles: short-term
goals are typically kept in cash-equivalent, low-risk accounts, since
there's little time to recover from a market downturn, while longer-term
goals have more room to take on investment risk in exchange for higher
expected returns.

Progress toward a goal is easiest to track when it's expressed as a
percentage of the target amount reached, alongside the amount and time
remaining — this makes it possible to tell early whether the current
contribution rate is actually on pace.

Competing goals (e.g. paying off debt vs. building an emergency fund)
are common; a frequent approach is to build a small starter emergency
fund first (to avoid new debt from unexpected costs), then prioritize
high-interest debt, then build the fund further and pursue other goals.
