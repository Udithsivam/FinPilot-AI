Expenses are usually split into fixed (rent, loan payments, insurance
premiums — roughly the same every period) and variable (groceries,
dining out, entertainment — fluctuating with behavior). Fixed expenses
are harder to change quickly, so most short-term expense reduction comes
from variable categories.

Categorizing transactions consistently (the same taxonomy every month)
is what makes month-over-month comparison meaningful — without it, a
spending increase can be hidden simply because similar purchases were
labeled differently in different months.

A sudden increase in a single category is more actionable to investigate
than a small drift across many categories, since it usually traces back
to a specific, identifiable change (a new subscription, a price
increase, a one-off large purchase) rather than a general shift in
habits.

Reviewing recurring charges periodically — subscriptions in particular —
is commonly recommended because they are easy to forget about once
set up, and their cumulative cost is often larger than any single
transaction would suggest.
