Two widely discussed strategies for paying down multiple debts are the
"avalanche" and "snowball" methods.

The debt avalanche method pays the minimum on all debts, then puts any
extra money toward the debt with the highest interest rate first. This
minimizes the total interest paid over time and is generally the
mathematically optimal approach.

The debt snowball method instead targets the smallest balance first,
regardless of interest rate, then rolls that payment into the next
smallest balance once it's paid off. It tends to pay slightly more in
interest overall, but the quick wins from closing out small debts can
help sustain motivation for people who struggle to stick with a
longer-term plan.

Consolidation (combining multiple debts into a single loan, often at a
lower rate) can simplify payments and reduce interest cost, but it can
also extend the repayment period or come with fees, so the total cost
should be compared before consolidating.

Missing payments has compounding costs beyond the immediate late fee: it
can trigger penalty interest rates and negatively affect credit scores,
which raises the cost of future borrowing.
