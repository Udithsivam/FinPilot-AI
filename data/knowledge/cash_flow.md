Personal cash flow is the net movement of money in and out of your
finances over a period: cash flow = income - expenses. Positive cash
flow means you took in more than you spent in that period; negative
cash flow means the opposite, funded by savings, credit, or a shortfall.

Cash flow is a *flow* concept (measured per period, like income
statements), distinct from net worth, which is a *stock* concept
(a snapshot of what you own minus what you owe at a point in time). It's
possible to have positive cash flow while net worth is falling (e.g. an
asset losing value) or negative cash flow in one month without an
underlying financial problem (e.g. a planned large purchase from
existing savings).

Because income and expenses aren't always evenly distributed within a
month, looking at a single month's cash flow in isolation can be
misleading; a rolling average over a few months is often more
representative of the underlying trend.

Consistently negative cash flow, if not addressed, depletes savings or
increases debt over time — the sustainable fix is either increasing
income, reducing expenses, or both, rather than treating it as
self-correcting.
