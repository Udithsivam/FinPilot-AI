Saving and investing are related but distinct: saving generally means
setting money aside in low-risk, easily accessible forms (cash, savings
accounts), while investing means putting money into assets (stocks,
bonds, mutual funds, real estate) with the expectation of growth, which
comes with the risk of loss.

Diversification means spreading money across different assets so that a
poor outcome in any single investment has a limited effect on the whole
portfolio. It doesn't eliminate risk, but it reduces the impact of any
one asset performing badly.

Compounding is growth on both the original amount and on previously
earned returns. Because of compounding, starting to invest earlier
generally matters more for the eventual outcome than the exact amount
invested each month, all else equal.

Risk and expected return are generally related: investments with higher
expected long-term returns (like equities) typically come with higher
short-term volatility than lower-risk options (like government bonds or
savings accounts). Matching that risk level to your time horizon and
tolerance for loss is a core part of investment planning.
